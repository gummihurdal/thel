Þel-lined salmon-leather glove

One product that shows both materials: salmon leather outside, soft þel wool inside. Cheap to prototype, ships in an envelope, and clears customs more easily than footwear.

Market. Luxury gloves sell at €150 to €400 from Italian and Scandinavian makers; it is a gift category, which suits a Reykjavík shop and the holiday season. Small market, but one a new brand can enter without permission.

Chance of success6 / 10

Low technical risk; the question is whether salmon leather stays supple in cold and wet use.

Cost to prove€8–12k

Pattern, 20 skins, two sample rounds and 150 pairs from a glove maker.

Retail€220

Landed cost about €50; gross margin near 75%.

Time to first sale5 months

Could be the first thing ÞEL sells.

Sell in Iceland first

Launch where the materials come from, to the people flying in to see the country. Buyers carry the brand home, so the first US customers arrive without import licences, tariffs or brokers.

Market. More than two million visitors a year, a large share from North America, passing through Keflavík and downtown Reykjavík with money set aside for something Icelandic. Current offer is dominated by lopapeysa sweaters and tourist shops; an elegant material-led store has no direct competitor.

Chance of success6 / 10

Demand is proven; the risk is rent and staffing a store from Switzerland.

Cost to prove€25k

A three-month summer pop-up. A permanent store fit-out runs about €90k.

Revenue potential€150–300k

Per year for a small central store, based on typical tourist-retail takings.

Time to first sale8 months

Summer 2027 pop-up.

Lifetime repair and buy-back

Free repair for life and a buy-back resale programme. Longevity is what the brand claims; this turns the claim into a contract and brings customers back.

Market. Resale and repair are growing faster than new luxury sales, and buyers at €400 and up increasingly expect them. For a small brand it is a loyalty tool more than a revenue line, but it raises conversion on the first purchase.

Chance of success7 / 10

Simple to run at small volumes; the risk is repair cost on fish leather.

Cost to prove€3–5k

A repair partner in Iceland and a page on the site.

Revenue potentialIndirect

Higher first-purchase conversion and repeat buyers.

Time to launchWith collection 1

Announce at launch, deliver from year two.

Fine þel knitwear

The core of the clothing line: crewnecks and cardigans spun from the inner coat only, in undyed natural colours, cut like nomerae or Toteme rather than like a lopapeysa.

Market. Premium knitwear is a large, crowded market where merino and cashmere set the reference. The opening is that nobody sells Icelandic wool as a fine, elegant yarn; the risk is that þel at 20 microns feels rustic next to cashmere at the same price.

Chance of success5 / 10

Depends entirely on the hand-feel test against merino and cashmere.

Cost to prove€35–50k

Yarn trial spin, designer, samples and a run of 250 pieces.

Retail€390–490

Landed cost €110 to €140.

Time to first sale10 months

Yarn trial must come first.

Provenance passport

An NFC tag in every piece opens the farm, the boat, the dye site and a photo of that exact item. It makes "no two alike" verifiable and blocks copies.

Market. As a feature it strengthens every other idea on this page. As a standalone product, small heritage brands and artisan makers lack affordable anti-counterfeit tools; the EU's coming digital product passport rules push the same way. A software founder could build it once and sell it twice.

Chance of success5 / 10

Near-certain as a feature; 3 as a standalone software business.

Cost to prove€8–15k

NFC tags at about €1 each, a web app, photography workflow.

Revenue potentialIndirect, then SaaS

Higher prices and trust first; licensing to other makers later.

Time to launchWith collection 1

Build in Q2 2027.

Earth-dyed cloth as a licensed material

Icelandic heather and birch tannin fixed with iron-rich volcanic earth on þel wool and silk, trademarked as a process and sold by the metre to other designers, the way Harris Tweed sells an ingredient.

Market. Luxury houses pay €300 and more per metre for rare heritage cloth; lotus fibre sells at about €300 a metre and gambiered silk garments at €450 to €940. The buyers are few but pay well, and an ingredient brand needs no stores. The science is published; the Icelandic version is unproven.

Chance of success4 / 10

Everything rests on the dye working, staying fast and passing lab tests.

Cost to prove€25–40k

Dye prototyping, fastness and function tests, trademark, 100 m pilot batch.

Revenue potentialHighest

Low fixed cost and 30 to 50% margins once the process is proven.

Time to first sale18–24 months

Garments first, metres to other brands in year two or three.

Arctic sneaker

Felted þel upper, salmon-leather overlays, natural rubber sole. The entry product that brings younger buyers to a heritage brand.

Market. Wool sneakers became a category worth hundreds of millions a year; luxury sneakers sell at €400 and up. Crowded, and footwear needs lasts, moulds and minimum orders that a small brand feels immediately. The salmon-leather story is strongest here, though.

Chance of success4 / 10

Strong idea, expensive category; needs a footwear partner with low minimums.

Cost to prove€40–60k

Lasts, tooling, samples and a 100-pair run in Portugal.

Retail€450

Landed cost about €160.

Time to first sale12 months

Second wave, after gloves and knitwear.

Nest-to-vest eiderdown

One hundred numbered eiderdown vests a year, each tied to a named eider farm, sold only from Iceland and by direct order. The halo that makes a €390 sweater look reasonable.

Market. Eiderdown duvets already sell at €4,000 and up, so the buyers exist. Ultra-luxury outerwear is tiny but tolerant of price. US resale of eiderdown sits in a regulatory grey zone, so this sells from Iceland and Europe first.

Chance of success4 / 10

Supply and price are fine; the US route and the sewing skill are the risks.

Cost to prove€20–25k

Down for 20 pieces, a specialist seamstress, certificates.

Retail€1,900

Landed cost about €650.

Time to first sale10 months

Launch with collection 1 as the showpiece.

Arctic gold: þel and muga

Undyed Icelandic greys paired with the natural gold of Assam muga silk: two colours as nature made them, no dye at all, co-branded with India's new muga export programme.

Market. Luxury scarves and accents; a press story more than a product line. India's programme is actively seeking export partners, which lowers sourcing cost, but it adds a second supply chain and a second set of import rules before the first one is proven.

Chance of success3 / 10

Good story, premature; revisit after collection 1 sells.

Cost to prove€8–12k

Muga yarn samples, 60 scarves, one shoot.

Retail€350

Estimated; muga yarn pricing still to confirm.

Time to first saleYear 2

Only after the core line is proven.
