Business plan, draft of October 2026

Own a material, not just a label.

ÞEL is a direct-to-consumer luxury label built on Icelandic materials, launched in Reykjavík and sold online to the United States and Europe. Wool for every garment, salmon leather for gloves and footwear, and an earth-dyed cloth nobody else makes. All figures below are estimates, not quotes.

The idea

Gambiered silk, the Chinese mud-and-sun cloth, sells at €450 to €940 a piece on a simple formula: a slow natural process, a visible trace of it on the surface, and no two lengths the same. Iceland has every ingredient for its own version. Black heather and birch bark give the tannin, iron-rich volcanic earth gives the mordant, and the Arctic light does the curing. Applied to the island's own fine wool and to silk, that makes a material no competitor can copy without years of work.

Around that signature cloth sit three more materials with deep supply and no elegant brand yet: þel, the soft inner coat of the Icelandic sheep; salmon and wolffish leather from the only fish tannery in Europe; and eiderdown, of which about three tonnes exist in the world each year, three quarters of it Icelandic.

Why it can win

  • A founder with real Icelandic roots, which is rare in fashion and impossible to fake.
  • Materials with proven supply: the wool mill, the tannery and the eiderdown farms already export.
  • Published lab studies show tannin-iron dyeing adds water resistance, antibacterial and flame-resistant properties, so the story has a function behind it.
  • A clear material split keeps the line simple: wool clothes you, salmon leather carries you.

Seven ideas with the most upside

  1. Icelandic earth-dyed cloth as an ownable, trademarked material, sold both as garments and by the metre to other designers.
  2. Sell in Iceland first. Two million visitors a year pass through Reykjavík and Keflavík; they carry the brand home with no import licences, tariffs or brokers.
  3. A provenance passport on every piece: an NFC tag opening the farm, the fjord, the dye site and a photo of that exact item.
  4. The þel-lined salmon-leather glove as the first product: cheap to prototype, easy to ship, shows both materials at once.
  5. The Arctic sneaker: felted þel upper, salmon-leather overlays, natural rubber sole.
  6. Nest-to-vest: one hundred numbered eiderdown vests a year, each tied to a named farm.
  7. A lifetime repair and buy-back programme, making longevity a contract rather than a slogan.

Customer and positioning

Women and men aged 35 to 60 in the United States and Europe who already buy minimalist, material-led brands, spend €300 to €1,500 per piece and care where things come from. Positioning: materials as nature made them, from the edge of the Arctic. Minimalist cuts; the material and its proof are the design.

Chances of success

An honest estimate, made before any prototype exists. Three outcomes, three scores.

OutcomeChance (0 to 10)Why
Sell a first capsule of about 200 pieces and break even on it6Real materials, a strong story, a captive tourist market in Reykjavík, and proof that a material-led label can launch on about €60,000. The risk is execution: the yarn must feel right at €390 and the earth dye must work and stay fast.
Build a profitable label with wholesale and US sales3Most fashion startups fail within three to five years. The survivors usually have a designer-founder, a fashion network and a marketing budget; none of these exist yet, the founder has a demanding day job, and fish leather and eiderdown add US regulatory friction a small brand cannot easily absorb.
Own the earth-dyed material and license it to other brands4Lower odds on its own, but the highest-value outcome. If the Icelandic dye works and lab tests confirm the properties, it is a defensible asset even if the clothing brand never scales, and it suits a technical, process-driven founder.

What moves the odds

  • A fashion or textile co-founder, ideally Icelandic, who owns design and production: the brand score moves from 3 toward 5.
  • The dye prototype working in the first two weekends. If it fails, the brand becomes another wool label and the score drops to 2.
  • 200 waiting-list signups before any production spend. Demand before stock is the cheapest insurance available.

The rule that follows: the next €10,000 buys an answer, not a business. Spin the yarn, test the dye, run the waiting list, and decide on the capsule only when all three come back positive.
